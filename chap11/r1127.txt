Let T be a red-black tree and let p be the position of the parent of the
original node that is deleted by the standard search tree deletion algorithm.
Prove that if p has one child, the deletion has caused a black deficit at p,
except for the case when the one remaining child is a red leaf.


Answer:
Let w denote the deleted node.
If p has one child after deletion, that
child cannot have come from w. If p
had as only child node w, which in turn had children
which become p child after deletion,
then node w would either violate red property
or the black depth property.

If deletion caused a black defiction at p,
then that means that p's child is black.
Hence the black depth and p's child will be
greater by 1 compared to the black depth at 
node p.

If p's child is red, then deletion of w
does not affect the black-depth property,
since p's child black depth is equal to p's.
(this also means that w was red to begin with).
P's child is also leaf, since a red node must
have black children, which would validate the
assumption that T is a valid red-black tree
before deletion (black depth at p's child
is greater than black depth at p).
