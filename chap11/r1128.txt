Let T be a red-black tree and let p be the position of the parent of the
original node that is deleted by the standard search tree deletion algorithm.
prove that if p has two children, the removed node was black and had one
red child.


Answer:

Let w denote the delete node.

Since p after deletion has two children,
then that means that w has exactly only child
which becomes p's child after deletion.
(If w had two children, BST swap would
precede the deletion, which would contradict
the premise that node w was deleted). 

Since T is a valid red-black tree, 
then w's child must have been red.
If it had been black, we would have a
black depth violation at w.
Since w's child is red, and T is valid
red-black tree, node w must have
been black to avoid a double red violation.
