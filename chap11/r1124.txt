Consider a tree T storing 100,000 entries. What is the worst-case height
of T in the following cases?
  a. T is a binary search tree.
  b. T is an AVL tree.
  c. T is a splay tree.
  d. T is a (2,4) tree.
  e. T is a red-black tree.


Answer: 
a) worst case in O(n) , i.e. height=100000
(tree is degraded to a linked list)


b) AVL trees have children with, at most,
a height difference of one in every node
position.

let n(h) be a function of the minimun number of 
nodes given height h in an AVL tree.

n(1) = 1
n(2) = 2
for h>=3:
n(h) = 1 + n(h-1) + n(h-2) (1)
=>
n(h) > 1 + 2*n(h-2) > 2n(h-2)

n(h) > 2n(h-2)
thus n(h) doubles as h grows by 2.

n(h) > 2^i*n(h-2i), where h - 2i >=1, i belongs in N

choose i such that h-2i = 1 or h-2i=2

i = h/2 + 1 if h is even or (h-1)/2 + 1 if h is odd

n(h) > 2^(h/2)*n(h - h +2)
n(h) > 2^(h/2)*n(2) = 2^(h+2/2)
logn > h+2/2 => h< 2logn -2 = 16,61*2 -2 = 31

or

n(h) > 2^(h+1/2)*n(h-h - 1 +2)=2^(h+1/2)*n(1)=2^(h+1/2)
logn > h+1/2
h < 2logn -1 = 32


Worst case height is 32
c) worst case is O(n), i.e. height=10000

since slay trees do not rebalance themselves.
